# Feasibility and Resource Landscape Report: Pediatric Oncology

## Executive Summary
Following the diagnosis of a rare condition like a Pineal Gland tumor, families are often thrust into a complex medical world with a steep learning curve. This report evaluates the current landscape of pediatric oncology resources in Canada and North America to justify the creation of a unified, educational open-source project.

## Existing Landscape (Successes to Imitate)
There are several strong resources currently available that we should use as benchmarks:
1. **Clinical Accuracy & Symptom Management:** Institutions like SickKids (AboutKidsHealth) and the Children's Oncology Group (COG) provide excellent, evidence-based guides on managing symptoms at home.
2. **Specialized Tumor Support:** The Brain Tumour Foundation of Canada and the Pediatric Brain Tumor Foundation (PBTF) offer specific handbooks for brain tumors, including support for siblings and school reentry.
3. **Regional Support Networks:** Organizations like POGO (Ontario), Leucan (Quebec), and Kids Cancer Care (Alberta) provide vital localized financial and emotional support.

## Caveats (Opportunities for Improvement)
Despite the excellent work of these organizations, several gaps remain that our project can address:
1. **Fragmentation of Information:** A family currently has to visit multiple distinct platforms to piece together medical facts (NCI), emotional support (PBTF), and local logistics (regional foundations). Our project can serve as a "Grand Unified" chronological roadmap.
2. **The "Rare Tumor" Gap:** For ultra-rare conditions like Pineal Gland tumors (less than 1% of pediatric brain tumors), general cancer foundation sites often lack deep specificity, forcing parents to parse dense, unapproachable clinical research papers. We can translate this high-level medical data into digestible, family-friendly formats.
3. **Navigational Overwhelm:** Newly diagnosed families suffer from information overload. Existing sites are often structured as encyclopedias rather than guided journeys. We can improve on this by creating a step-by-step UI/UX that walks parents from Day 1 (Diagnosis) through Treatment and into Survivorship.

## Project Viability & Justification
Creating a centralized, open-source educational repository is highly viable and necessary. By leveraging modern web frameworks and AI-assisted fact-checking, we can create a platform that curates and demystifies the pediatric oncology journey, filling the specific gap of rare tumor education and unified navigation.
