Neutropenia occurs when a child has an abnormally low number of neutrophils, a type of white blood cell essential for fighting off bacterial infections. During chemotherapy, white blood cell counts can drop significantly, leaving the body highly vulnerable.

When a child with neutropenia develops a fever (typically defined as a single oral temperature of 101°F / 38.3°C or higher, or a sustained temperature of 100.4°F / 38.0°C for more than one hour), it is considered **a medical emergency**.

## Do Not Manage at Home

Unlike a common childhood fever, **neutropenic fever cannot and should not be managed at home**. Because your child's immune system is compromised, an infection can become life-threatening rapidly.

### Immediate Steps to Take:

1. **Seek Emergency Care Immediately:** Contact your child's oncology team or go to the nearest emergency department right away, exactly as instructed by your care team.
2. **Do Not Wait:** Even if the fever seems low or your child appears relatively well and stable, do not wait for the fever to go down on its own. Do not wait until the next business day.
3. **Do Not Give Fever-Reducing Medications:** Do not give acetaminophen (Tylenol), ibuprofen (Advil/Motrin), or any other fever-reducing medications unless explicitly told to do so by your oncology team. These medications can mask the fever and delay life-saving treatment.
4. **Follow Your Care Plan:** Always refer back to the specific emergency protocols and contact numbers provided by your child's oncology team at treating hospitals like St. Jude, SickKids, or your local center.

> **Always remember:** A fever in a child receiving cancer treatment requires urgent professional medical attention.
