# Chemo-Induced Nausea & Vomiting (CINV)

Chemotherapy-induced nausea and vomiting (CINV) are among the most common and distressing side effects of pediatric cancer treatment. Managing CINV usually requires a multidisciplinary approach, combining prescription medications (anti-emetics) with supportive, non-pharmacological care at home.

## At-Home Management and Support

Your child's oncology team will prescribe specific anti-nausea medications based on their treatment protocol. It is vital to administer these exactly as prescribed, often on a schedule, rather than waiting for nausea to start. In addition to these medications, you can support your child at home with the following strategies:

### 1. Dietary Adjustments
*   **Small, Frequent Meals:** Offer small portions of food throughout the day instead of three large meals. An empty stomach can sometimes make nausea worse.
*   **Bland Foods:** Stick to bland, easy-to-digest foods like crackers, toast, plain rice, and broth. 
*   **Avoid Strong Odors:** Cooking smells, heavy perfumes, and strong odors can trigger nausea. Serve foods at room temperature or cold to reduce their smell.

### 2. Integrative Medicine Approaches
Many pediatric oncology centers incorporate complementary techniques to alleviate CINV. Always consult your oncology team before trying these at home:
*   **Acupressure:** Applying gentle pressure to specific points (such as the P6 point on the inner wrist) can help alleviate nausea. Caregivers can often be taught these techniques for younger children.
*   **Acupuncture:** Some clinics offer acupuncture as an adjunct therapy for older children and teens.

### 3. Mind-Body Techniques
Helping your child relax can reduce both anticipatory nausea (nausea that occurs before treatment begins) and anxiety:
*   **Clinical Hypnosis:** Guided imagery and clinical hypnosis are evidence-based, non-pharmacological interventions that help patients cope with physical symptoms and distress.
*   **Distraction:** Engaging in calm activities, such as listening to music, reading a book, or watching a favorite movie, can help shift focus away from the nausea.

## When to Call for Help
Monitor your child closely and contact your oncology team's 24-hour triage line if you observe:
*   An inability to keep liquids or medications down.
*   Signs of dehydration (decreased urination, dry mouth, crying without tears, extreme lethargy).
*   Breakthrough vomiting that does not improve after taking prescribed anti-nausea medications.
