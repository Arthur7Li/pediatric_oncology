Cancer and its treatments can cause significant pain for children. It is a fundamental right of every child to have their pain recognized and treated effectively. In Canadian pediatric oncology centers, pain management is a top priority, and medical teams have advanced tools to keep your child comfortable.

## Understanding Pediatric Pain

Children may experience pain from the tumor itself, from surgical procedures, or as a side effect of treatments (like chemotherapy-induced neuropathy or mucositis).

Because young children may not have the words to describe their pain, hospitals use specialized, age-appropriate pain scales (like the FACES scale) to help children communicate their comfort level.

## The 3P Approach to Pain Management

Leading institutions like SickKids utilize the **"3P" approach** to ensure pain is tackled from all angles:

1. **Physical:** Using physical strategies to relieve pain, such as heat packs, cold packs, gentle massage, physiotherapy, and positioning.
2. **Psychological:** Helping the brain cope with pain signals. This includes deep breathing, distraction (video games, VR headsets during procedures), guided imagery, and maintaining comforting daily routines.
3. **Pharmacological:** The use of medications, ranging from over-the-counter pain relievers to strong prescription medications.

## The WHO Analgesic Ladder

When medications are needed, pediatric oncologists generally follow principles adapted from the World Health Organization (WHO) analgesic ladder. This involves a stepwise approach based on the severity of the pain:

- **Mild Pain:** Treated with non-opioid medications like Acetaminophen (Tylenol) or NSAIDs (like Ibuprofen/Advil). _Note: NSAIDs are often restricted if platelet counts are low or kidney function is compromised._
- **Moderate to Severe Pain:** Treated with opioid analgesics. Morphine is the gold standard for severe pediatric cancer pain.

### Key Principles of Medication

- **"By the Clock":** For ongoing, continuous pain, medications are scheduled at regular intervals (by the clock) rather than given only "as needed." This prevents the pain from returning and keeps the child consistently comfortable.
- **Oral First:** Whenever possible, pain medications are given by mouth (liquid or pills) as it is the least invasive route.
- **Don't Fear Addiction:** When opioids (like morphine) are used appropriately for cancer pain under medical supervision, the risk of psychological addiction in children is exceedingly low. Do not withhold necessary pain medication out of fear of addiction.

## 🚨 When to Call the Care Team (Red Flags)

While some pain is expected during treatment (like mouth sores or bone pain from growth factor injections), certain types of pain require immediate medical evaluation.

**Seek emergency medical attention or page your oncology team immediately if:**

- **Pain is accompanied by a fever** (38.0°C / 100.4°F or higher).
- **Pain is sudden and extremely severe** (e.g., a sudden, blinding headache or excruciating abdominal pain).
- **New pain develops** that your care team has not warned you about.
- **Pain is uncontrolled**, meaning the current medication plan is no longer providing relief.
- The pain is associated with **new weakness, numbness, or loss of bowel/bladder control** (which can indicate pressure on the spinal cord).
