Mucositis refers to the inflammation and ulceration of the mucous membranes lining the mouth, throat, and gastrointestinal tract. It is a common and painful side effect of certain chemotherapy drugs and radiation therapy.

While mucositis can cause significant discomfort, there are several empathetic and actionable ways to help your child manage symptoms at home.

## At-Home Care and Management

### 1. Alkaline Saline Mouth Rinse

Rinsing can help reduce mouth acid, keep the tissues moist, promote healing, and make eating and talking more comfortable.

- **Recipe:** Mix 4 cups of warm water, 1 teaspoon of table salt, and 1 teaspoon of baking soda.
- **How to Use:** Have your child swish and gargle with about 15 mL (1 tablespoon) for 15–30 seconds, then spit it out. **Do not swallow the rinse.**
- **Frequency:** Use every 4–6 hours, or as directed by your care team. Store at room temperature and discard after 14 days.

### 2. Gentle Oral Hygiene

Maintaining oral hygiene is critical to prevent infection.

- Use an extra-soft-bristle toothbrush to avoid irritating the gums and sores.
- If your child finds brushing too painful, speak with your care team about alternatives, such as using specialized swabs.

### 3. Dietary Adjustments

Transition to foods that are gentle on the mouth and easy to swallow:

- **Choose Soft Foods:** Opt for cooked and tender foods like scrambled eggs, mashed potatoes, cooked/canned fruits, and soft vegetables (e.g., well-cooked green beans).
- **Avoid Irritants:** Avoid foods that are acidic (citrus, tomatoes), spicy, or hard and crunchy, as these can scrape and sting open sores.
- **Limit Sugar:** Sugary drinks and snacks can promote bacteria and poor oral health.

### 4. Stay Hydrated

Mucositis can make swallowing painful, increasing the risk of dehydration. Offer small, frequent sips of cool water or clear broths. Popsicles or ice chips can also be soothing and hydrating.

## When to Contact Your Care Team

Always notify your pediatric oncology team if you notice:

- Soreness or pain on the lips, mouth, or throat.
- Difficulty swallowing or noticeably increased drooling.
- New white patches or sores in the mouth.
- A significant decrease in your child's ability to eat or drink.
