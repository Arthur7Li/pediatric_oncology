## Overview

Pineal tumors are rare, accounting for only 3-8% of pediatric brain tumors. They occur in or around the pineal gland, a small organ deep in the brain.

## Specific Types

- **Germinoma:** The most common type of pineal region tumor and highly treatable with radiation and chemotherapy.
- **Pineoblastoma:** A rare, aggressive, and fast-growing tumor.
- **Pineocytoma:** Rare in children, this is typically a slow-growing (benign) tumor.

## Specific Symptoms

Because of their location, these tumors often block the flow of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF), leading to:

- **Hydrocephalus:** Fluid buildup in the brain causing severe headaches, nausea, and vomiting.
- **Parinaud Syndrome:** Difficulty looking upward and unusual eye movements.

## Diagnosis & Standard Treatment

- **Diagnosis:** Often involves MRIs to view the tumor, a Lumbar Puncture (spinal tap) to check for tumor markers in the spinal fluid, and potentially a biopsy.
- **Treatment:** A combination of Surgery (to relieve fluid buildup and remove the tumor if possible), Radiation, and Chemotherapy.
