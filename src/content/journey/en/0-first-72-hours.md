1. **What's Happening Right Now** — Your child has just been diagnosed or is being evaluated. This is overwhelming, but you are not alone. Your medical team is already working.

2. **Tests to Expect** — Blood work (CBC, chemistry panel), imaging (CT, MRI, or ultrasound depending on the tumor type), biopsy or bone marrow aspirate if needed. These help the team understand exactly what they're dealing with.

3. **Your Immediate Medical Team** — You'll meet your pediatric oncologist, nurses, a social worker, and likely a child life specialist. Each plays a vital role.

4. **What to Ask Your Doctor** — Key questions: What type of cancer does my child have? What are the next steps? How long will we be in hospital? Is there a clinical trial available?

5. **Telling Your Child** — Age-appropriate guidance (toddlers vs. school-age vs. teens). Use simple, honest language. Let the child life specialist help.

6. **Telling Your Family** — You don't have to tell everyone right away. Focus on your immediate support circle first. Consider designating one person to relay updates.

7. **Immediate Financial Steps** — Contact your employer about leave. Ask the social worker about EI Family Caregiver Benefits. Gather insurance documents.

8. **What to Pack** — Reference the checklists page.

9. **Taking Care of Yourself** — You cannot pour from an empty cup. Accept help. Sleep when you can. Eat something. This is a marathon, not a sprint.
