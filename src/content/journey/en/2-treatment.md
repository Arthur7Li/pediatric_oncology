Once the diagnosis and stage are clear, a multidisciplinary medical team (which includes pediatric oncologists, surgeons, and nurses) will create a personalized treatment plan. The plan may involve one or a combination of the following:

*   **Chemotherapy ("Chemo"):** The use of powerful medicines to kill cancer cells or stop them from growing. Chemo travels throughout the entire body, so it can reach cancer cells that might have spread. 
*   **Surgery:** A physical operation to remove the tumor and some surrounding healthy tissue to ensure no cancer cells are left behind in that area. 
*   **Radiation Therapy:** The use of high-energy rays (similar to strong X-rays) targeted precisely at the tumor to destroy cancer cells and shrink tumors. 
*   **Stem Cell Transplant (Bone Marrow Transplant):** A procedure used when higher-than-normal doses of chemotherapy or radiation are needed. Because high doses can severely damage the bone marrow, healthy stem cells (which grow into new blood cells) are infused back into the child's body to replace the damaged marrow.

During this phase, doctors often rely on treatment protocols designed by the Children's Oncology Group (COG), a global organization that sets the standard of care for childhood cancers through clinical trials and research.
