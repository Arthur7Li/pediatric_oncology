The journey often begins when parents notice persistent, unusual symptoms—such as unexplained pain, lingering fevers, unusual bruising, or new lumps—and seek medical care.

When a doctor suspects cancer, they will perform tests to confirm the diagnosis and understand the disease fully:

- **Scans (Imaging):** Doctors use special cameras to take detailed pictures of the inside of the body. Common scans include X-rays, Ultrasounds, CT scans (Computed Tomography), and MRIs (Magnetic Resonance Imaging). These help locate the tumor and see its size.
- **Biopsies:** A biopsy is a procedure where a doctor removes a very small piece of the tumor or a sample of bone marrow (the spongy center of bones where blood is made). This sample is looked at under a microscope to find out exactly what kind of cancer cells are present.
- **Staging:** Staging is a way doctors describe how large a tumor is and whether the cancer has spread from where it started to other parts of the body. Knowing the exact "stage" helps the medical team design the safest and most effective treatment plan.
