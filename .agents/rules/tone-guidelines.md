# Rule: Empathetic & Accessible Tone

1. **Avoid Clinical Coldness:** Translate dense medical jargon into accessible language without losing accuracy. For example, explain "pineoblastoma" or "hydrocephalus" in simple, parent-friendly terms.
2. **Compassionate but Objective:** Maintain a supportive tone, acknowledging the difficulty of the journey, but avoid being overly dramatic or fatalistic. Focus on actionable information and hope.
3. **Structured for the Overwhelmed:** Assume the reader is sleep-deprived and stressed. Use bullet points, bold text for key terms, and short paragraphs to make information easily scannable.
